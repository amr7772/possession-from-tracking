How it works

The question is simple: at every moment of a football match, does the home team have the ball, does the away team have it, or is the ball dead? The model answers it from positions alone, the 22 players and the ball, sampled five times a second. The event log is used to build the answer key and to score the model, never as an input.

The answer key

A team gets the ball at each pass, shot, recovery, lost ball or set piece the event log credits it with, and keeps it until the other team's next one or until the ball goes dead. The ball goes dead when it crosses a line, when the referee stops play for a foul or an offside that leads to a set piece, and at the end of each half. Metrica only tracks the ball while it is in play, so a gap of more than five seconds with no ball and nothing in the log counts as a stoppage too.

What the model looks at

About eighty numbers per frame: how far the nearest home and away players are from the ball, and how that gap moved over the previous and next few seconds; the speed of the ball and the sudden changes in its velocity that mark a touch, with the team that was closest at each touch; whether the ball is tracked at all or sits outside the lines; how fast the players are moving; and the shape of each team.

The model

A gradient-boosted tree classifier (LightGBM) scores each frame. A three-state hidden Markov model then picks the most likely sequence, which keeps the answer from flickering between teams every time two players are level with the ball. Its two settings were tuned on game 1 alone. Game 2 was only used for the final score.

How it is scored

Frame macro-F1 averages the F1 of home, away and dead ball over every frame. Spell F1 looks at possession spells, unbroken runs of one team on the ball. A predicted spell counts as found when it overlaps a true spell of the same team with an intersection-over-union of at least 0.5. A prediction that flickers breaks spells into pieces, which is why the nearest-player baseline does fine on frames and badly on spells.

Limits

Two matches, both anonymised and from the same provider, so this says nothing yet about other leagues or other tracking systems. The answer key inherits the event log's timing, which can be a few frames off. And because the ball stops being tracked when play stops, spotting dead balls is the easy half of the problem here; telling home from away is where the work is.
